LGD Desk methodology

The lab-grown diamond counterpart of STTIE (yarn) and Gold Desk, built on the same code, data rules and learning loop.

1. Who it is for and what it answers

  • Audience: Surat lab-grown diamond (LGD) manufacturers (CVD/HPHT growers, cutters and polishers) and polished LGD traders, plus their bankers.
  • The one question: this month, should I stock up on LGD (rough or polished), wait, or buy only part (what confirmed orders need)?
  • Why monthly: the only free, official, regularly published LGD figures are GJEPC's monthly export reviews. There is no free daily or weekly LGD price. So the call changes once a month, when a new GJEPC report appears (usually the 7th–15th of the next month). The site checks daily so a new report is picked up within a day. It does not invent daily signals.
  • Gujarati-first one-screen view with Hindi and English, WhatsApp share and read-aloud. Details in English.
  • Not investment advice. Decision support from public data only.

2. Principles

  • No invented data. Every number is fetched from a named public source with its date, is a statutory rate checked by hand with its link and date, is arithmetic on those numbers with the formula shown, or is your own entry.
  • Where no free source exists the tile stays grey (rough LGD prices, Surat trade prices). Unofficial sources are labelled unofficial.
  • No AI produces any number, call, weight or translation. Text is fixed templates. Headlines can only soften a 'stock up' call to 'buy part'.
  • Zero running cost: static GitHub Pages plus one GitHub Actions run a day (10:00 IST). No keys, no backend, no login.

3. Data

  • GJEPC monthly export review (gjepc.org/statistics.php, public PDFs, official, provisional): the "Polished Lab Grown Diamonds" text section gives the month's export value and the same month last year, the April-to-date value and last year's, and (since Sep 2025) April-to-date carats with last year's carats and the price per carat. Only the PDF's text layer is read; the image annex (monthly carats, rough LGD imports) is not OCR'd. Each month uses its own report (first-published figures). GJEPC later reconciles with DGCI&S and restates last year's figures, sometimes by a lot (carats especially).
  • Caratlytics Diamond Price Index (CC BY 4.0, unofficial): monthly median listed price per carat of lab-grown stones at 140+ mostly US online retailers, since Dec 2025. Asking prices, not trade prices; mix effects. Shown for context; not used in the call.
  • US BLS: CPI for jewelry (CUUR0000SEAG02) and jobs at jewelry, luggage and leather stores (CEU4245830001). FRED IR413: US import price index for gems, jewelry and coins (mostly natural diamonds and gold; context only).
  • USD/INR: FBIL reference rate from the RBI homepage; ECB monthly averages (Frankfurter) for history.
  • Checked by hand (data/rates.json): US Section 301 duty of 10% on goods of India from 24 Jul 2026 (Federal Register 91 FR 47318), base US duty 0% on HTS 7104.91.10, 12.5% on most rival origins; DGVCL HTP-I energy charge ₹4.00/4.20/4.30 per kWh from 1 Apr 2026 (GERC tariff schedule).
  • Headlines: Google News RSS, context only.
  • Not available free: rough LGD prices and rough import volumes (images only in GJEPC reports; DGCI&S/TradeStat data for Apr–Oct 2026 not yet released), Surat polished trade prices (use the quote tracker on the main screen), US Census HS 7104 imports (API needs a key).

4. Gauges and formulas

  • Month carats = this report's April-to-date carats − the previous month's report's April-to-date carats (April: the April figure itself). Only same-financial-year, consecutive reports are differenced; otherwise the month stays blank.
  • Export price per carat (month) = month export value ÷ month carats. It moves with the mix of sizes and qualities as well as with price.
  • Price pressure = this year's April-to-date price per carat ÷ last year's for the same months, as stated in the same report, − 1.
  • Demand = last 3 months' export value ÷ the same 3 months last year (as stated in those reports) − 1.

5. Rules and the call

  • Price rule (default 5%): price pressure ≤ −5% → wait; ≥ +5% → stock up. Demand rule (default 15%): demand ≥ +15% → stock up; ≤ −15% → wait. Both editable.
  • Both agree → that action; they disagree or neither fires → buy part.
  • Each rule side is back-tested against what happened next: the following month's GJEPC export price per carat (up: stock up right; down: wait right; buy part right within ±3%). A side drives the call only with at least 12 scored signals and a better record than doing the opposite. Until then the call is 'buy part' and the page says the data is too thin.
  • A recent headline about falling prices, a glut or a price war can soften 'stock up' to 'buy part'; it can never create a call.

6. Forecast, learning and the ledger

  • Next month's export value (demand): ridge least squares on momentum, 3-month YoY growth, US jewelry CPI 3-month change and USD/INR 1-month change, re-fitted each run. Walk-forward back-test from 24 months of warm-up, each forecast fitted only on earlier months. A variable keeps weight only if alone it cuts walk-forward error by at least 1% versus 'no change', and the kept set must beat 'no change'.
  • Next month's price per carat: only about ten month-to-month pairs exist, so the forecast is 'no change' with zero weights until 24 months are available.
  • Each new GJEPC month the app logs a dated set of predictions (export value, price per carat, call) in data/predictions.json with the model version, and scores them when the next report arrives. data/track_record.csv, data/models/ (archived versions) and the changelog in data/model.json keep the history.
